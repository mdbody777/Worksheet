  4. Section D:
    1. H. contortus attaches to the abomasal mucosa and actively feeds on blood, causing severe blood loss and subsequent anemia.
    2. Larvae migrate from the liver to the lungs, are coughed up the trachea, swallowed, and finally mature into adults in the small intestine.
    3. It breaks the life cycle by moving hosts away from pastures contaminated with infective L3 larvae before they can be ingested.
    4. It indicates the presence of live nematode larvae (e.g., lungworms) in the feces, rather than eggs, which require specific isolation methods.
    5. Hookworms attach to the intestinal mucosa and feed directly on the host's blood; roundworms swim freely in the lumen and feed on intestinal contents (digesta).
